U1-242 marks

Distinguish a capital receipt from a revenue receipt.

Answer

A capital receipt comes from the sale or loss of a fixed asset or source of income (sale of machinery, loan received) and is not taxable unless a specific provision taxes it (e.g. capital gains). A revenue receipt arises from the normal use of assets or business operations (sales, rent, interest) and is taxable.

U1-4310 marks

Explain the rules for determining the residential status of an individual under sec 6, including the exceptions and the conditions for "ordinarily resident".

Answer

Step 1 — basic conditions (satisfy any one): (a) in India 182 days or more in the PY; or (b) 60 days or more in the PY and 365 days or more in the 4 preceding years.

Exceptions to (b): the 60 days becomes 182 for (i) an Indian citizen leaving India for employment or as crew of an Indian ship; (ii) an Indian citizen or person of Indian origin coming on a visit — but 120 days if his Indian income exceeds ₹15 lakh.

Deemed resident (6(1A)): citizen with Indian income above ₹15 lakh, not liable to tax elsewhere.

Step 2 — ROR if both: resident in at least 2 of the 10 preceding years and in India 730 days or more in the 7 preceding years. Otherwise RNOR. A 120-day visitor and a deemed resident are always RNOR.

Significance: ROR — global income; RNOR — Indian income + foreign income from business controlled / profession set up in India; NR — Indian income only. Status is determined afresh every year and separately for each PY.
