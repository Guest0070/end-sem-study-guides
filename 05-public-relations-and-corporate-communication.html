Public Relations & Corporate Communication

Revision notes built around the press-release and strategic-communication assignments in this folder.

PR manages mutually beneficial relationships between an organisation and its publics. Corporate communication coordinates what an organisation says and does across employees, customers, media, investors, government and communities.

2. The PR planning process

  1. Research: What is the situation? What evidence and perceptions exist?
  2. Objectives: Specific, measurable, audience-focused and time-bound.
  3. Publics: Segment stakeholders; do not treat “the public” as one audience.
  4. Strategy: The broad approach; explain why it will work.
  5. Tactics: Concrete actions/channels—media briefing, event, newsletter, creator partnership.
  6. Timeline, budget and responsibilities.
  7. Evaluation: Measure outputs, outtakes and outcomes.
Example objective: “Within eight weeks, increase awareness of the campus refill station among first-year residents from the baseline survey level to 70%.” This is better than “create awareness.”

3. Stakeholders, message and channels

Map stakeholders by interest and influence. High-influence/high-interest stakeholders need close engagement; low-interest groups may need monitoring. A message should have one clear core idea, evidence, audience relevance and a call to action. Match channel to audience: media release for journalists, FAQ for customers, town hall for employees, investor presentation for investors.

4. Press-release writing

A press release is a factual, newsworthy announcement written for journalists. Use the inverted pyramid: most important information first, then context, then detail.

HEADLINE: clear, specific, newsworthy DATELINE: City, date — LEAD: Who did what, where, when and why it matters. BODY: key facts, evidence, relevant quote, background. BOILERPLATE: 2–3 lines about the organisation. MEDIA CONTACT: name, email, phone.

Use plain language, active verbs, verified facts and attributed quotes. Avoid unsupported superlatives (“best”, “revolutionary”), vague claims and promotional clutter.

5. Crisis communication

A crisis threatens people, operations or reputation and demands timely, accurate communication. Priorities: protect people, establish facts, appoint a spokesperson, communicate what is known/unknown, give actions and update regularly. Do not speculate, hide confirmed facts or blame before investigation.

First holding statement: acknowledge the incident, express concern, state immediate action, identify where updates will appear and give a media contact. It is not the final investigation report.

Ethical PR requires truthfulness, transparency, respect for privacy, disclosure of conflicts, fair representation and correction of material errors. Reputation is built through conduct as well as communication.

Practice questions

1. Give one difference between a strategy and a tactic.

Strategy is the broad approach to meet an objective; a tactic is a specific activity used to execute it.

2. Rewrite “increase awareness” as a measurable objective.

Include audience, baseline/target, timeframe and method—for example, “raise aided awareness among X from 35% to 60% by December, measured by survey.”

3. What information belongs in the lead paragraph of a press release?

The key news: who, what, when, where, why/how it matters.

4. Why are media impressions alone weak evaluation?

They show possible exposure, not whether the right people understood, trusted or acted on the message.

5. Draft the first sentence of a holding statement after a product-safety complaint.

Example: “We are aware of reports concerning [product], are treating customer safety as our priority, and have begun an immediate review with the relevant teams.”
