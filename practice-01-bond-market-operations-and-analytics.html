U1-275 marks

Explain the process by which the Government of India borrows in the primary market.

Answer
  1. Budget fixes gross borrowing; RBI and GOI publish the half-yearly issuance calendar.
  2. RBI notifies each auction (security, amount, auction type).
  3. Bids on RBI's E-Kuber: competitive (banks, PDs, institutions), non-competitive (retail via Retail Direct, up to 5%).

U1-2910 marks

Explain the role of repo markets in liquidity management, with the RBI's liquidity adjustment framework. (10)

Answer

Repo = collateralised borrowing; features: collateral (G-Secs, T-Bills, SDLs, corporate bonds), haircut, tenor (overnight to term), rate. Market repo (bilateral) and TREPS (tri-party, CCIL). U1-3015-mark case · numerical

Case (15 marks). A mid-sized bank's treasury must (i) bid in a ₹10,000 crore 10-year G-Sec auction, (ii) manage a temporary shortfall of ₹500 crore for 3 days, and (iii) invest surplus funds for 3 months.

(a) Explain how it should bid and which auction format favours it (5). (b) Recommend how to raise the 3-day funds and compute the cost at a 6.5% repo rate with a 2% haircut (5). (c) Recommend instruments for the 3-month surplus (5).

Answer

(a) Bid competitively on E-Kuber at yields based on the secondary-market yield curve and when-issued trading; shade bids to avoid the winner's curse. Uniform-price auctions reduce winner's-curse risk (all pay the cut-off); multiple-price auctions reward precise bidding.

(b) Use market repo/TREPS (or RBI VRR) against surplus SLR G-Secs; collateral needed = 500 ÷ 0.98 = ₹510.20 crore; interest = 500 × 6.5% × 3/365 = ₹0.2671 crore (≈ ₹26.71 lakh). MSF only as last resort (higher rate).

(c) 91-day T-Bills (sovereign, liquid), CDs of highly rated banks, CPs of AAA/A1+ corporates for a spread, or reverse repo/TREPS lending; match maturity to the need.
